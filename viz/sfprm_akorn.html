Fixed-point reasoning on the sphere

SFPRM is an AKOrN with zero natural frequency

Every operation in an SFPRM iteration (geodesic injection, nGPT sub-layers, geodesic damping) moves a unit vector along a great circle toward a tangent direction. That is exactly the Kuramoto update used by AKOrN. This page starts with the basic concepts, then shows the identity, how one iteration splits into AKOrN steps, and an earlier proposal built on small oscillators with random restarts. The last section shows the new method: an energy-based SFPRM whose iterations are gradient steps on an explicit energy with a codebook memory, with toy demos and the measured results.

Basics, interactively

Four ideas carry the rest of the page: splitting a vector into radial and tangential parts, oscillators that synchronize, an oscillator that locks to a drive, and damping as a way to tame rotating modes. Each figure matches a subsection of Section 2 in the LaTeX note.

1 · Radial and tangential parts, and why normalization only sees the tangent

Any update v at a point z splits into ⟨v,z⟩z (radial) and Pzv (tangential). After normalizing, only the direction of z + v survives, so the radial part just rescales the tangential step (Lemma A). The normalization step and the exponential map move along the same great circle, by arctan‖Pzv‖ and ‖Pzv‖ respectively.

2 · The Kuramoto model: coupling against frequency spread

Forty phase oscillators with natural frequencies drawn from a normal distribution of width σ, all coupled with strength K: θ̇i = ωi + (K/n) Σj sin(θj − θi). Below a critical coupling (about 1.6σ for this distribution) the phases stay spread out. Above it, a synchronized cluster forms and the order parameter r rises toward 1.

3 · Locking to a drive (the Adler condition)

A single oscillator with natural frequency ω pulled by a fixed drive h: θ̇ = ω − ‖h‖ sin(θ − φh). If the drive beats the frequency, the oscillator locks at a constant lag behind h, which is a fixed point. Otherwise it keeps slipping round. This is the stimulus term of AKOrN and the injection of SFPRM, and it is the condition the oscillatory extension of SFPRM has to satisfy to halt.

4 · Damping a fixed-point iteration

Near a fixed point, the error follows the Jacobian: each eigenvalue ζ multiplies its mode every step. Damping with step η replaces ζ by 1 + η(ζ − 1), a point on the segment from 1 to ζ. The mode decays if that point is inside the unit circle. Rotating modes (large imaginary part) need small η; modes with Re ζ ≥ 1 cannot be rescued.

The two update rules

AKOrN step (oscillators on SN−1) xi ← Π( xi + γ [ Ωixi + Pxi( ci + Σj Jijxj ) ] )
SFPRM iteration (tokens on Sd−1) z̃ = fθ( Slerpλ(z, x) ),   z ← Slerpη(z, z̃)

Here Π(y) = y/‖y‖, Pzv = v − ⟨v,z⟩z projects onto the tangent space, Ω is antisymmetric (the natural frequency), J the coupling and c the stimulus. Two lemmas connect them:

Lemma A · renormalized residual step Π(z + w) = Π( z + Pzw / (1 + ⟨w,z⟩) )
Lemma B · Slerp Slerpη(u,v) = Expu(η Loguv) = Π( u + tan(ηθ)/sin θ · Puv )

Applied to the damping step, Lemma B gives the exact statement: one SFPRM iteration is z ← Π(z + γ PzFθ(z;x)) with γ = tan(ηθ)/sin θ. That is an AKOrN step with Ω = 0, one oscillator per token (N = d), and the whole recurrent map as its drive.

One step, two forms

Everything happens in the plane spanned by the state z and the proposal z̃, so a circle shows the whole construction. Slerp rotates z by ηθ. The AKOrN form walks along the tangent line by γ Pzz̃ and renormalizes. Both land on the same point. Only the step size differs between update rules; nGPT-style lerp-then-normalize lands elsewhere on the same arc, with its own γ.

Inside one iteration: operator-split AKOrN

Lemma A applies to every nGPT sub-layer and Lemma B to the injection, so each stage of the recurrent map is its own AKOrN step, with its own drive.

AKOrN sums stimulus and coupling into one Euler step. SFPRM applies them one after another. When the step sizes are small (λ, α = O(ε)), the sequence collapses to a single AKOrN step with drive c + Jz + m(z), with an error of order ε². A constant Ω adds nothing new: oscillatory SFPRM with block F equals vanilla SFPRM with block F′(z) = Expz(Ωz + LogzF(z)), for every η. What cannot be folded into the block is a change of state space or anything that varies across iterations, which is what the proposal below uses.

Earlier proposal: small oscillators with random restarts

Split each token into M = d/N small oscillators on SN−1, keep the nGPT block unchanged, and add EqR-style random initialization, path noise and restarts. The halt doubles as a certificate, since 99.7–100% of halted Sudoku puzzles are solved in the SFPRM experiments.

Update, per oscillator group z ← Expz( ηi Logz Π(z̃) + βi ξ ),   ξ ~ N(0, Pz),   βi = β0ηi/η0,   z0 ~ Uniform(SN−1)

With β₀ = 0 and N = d this is exactly SFPRM. Tying the noise to the damping means the patience rule anneals both together. Run B restarts, accept the first that halts, and fall back to the lowest residual or anchor angle.

Why small oscillators: random starts are not random on a big sphere

On S511 a random state sits at 90° ± 2.5° from any fixed input vector, so every restart starts at the same anchor angle, which is the quantity injection acts on. Per group of size N the starting angle is spread out.

The mechanism on a toy: two oscillators, four attractors

Two N = 2 oscillators live on a torus. A toy potential has one narrow, deep well (the solution) and three broad, shallower wells (wrong attractors). Each restart runs the SFPRM solver: a gradient-step proposal, geodesic damping with the patience rule, tangent noise tied to η, and chordal halting. This is a toy, not a trained model; it shows the mechanics of breadth and noise, not their size on Sudoku.

New method: an energy-based SFPRM with a codebook memory

The dictionary has one row where the two models disagree: AKOrN can have an energy, SFPRM never does. The new method closes that gap from the SFPRM side. Each iteration takes one Riemannian gradient step on an explicit energy, so every step is an AKOrN step whose drive is −grad E, and the halting residual becomes the size of that gradient.

The figures below build the intuition in four steps: what an energy does to the dynamics near a fixed point, what the memory term does to a single cell, a small Sudoku solved by descending an energy, and what the trained models show.

1 · An energy removes rotation, but not stiffness

Near a halted state the error e evolves as e ← (I − ηM)e. For a gradient step, M is the Hessian of E: symmetric, so its eigenvalues are real and the error shrinks without turning. A learned map like SFPRM's adds an antisymmetric part ωR, which rotates the error. Rotation pushes the eigenvalues off the real axis and lets the energy rise along the way. The stiffness slider makes one mode steeper than the other, which is what the trained energy models turned out to be.

2 · One cell: the memory term and its β knob

With prototypes of equal norm, eβ⟨ξk, z⟩ is a von Mises–Fisher density on the sphere, so the memory energy is minus the log of a mixture centred on the prototypes (green ring). Here a single cell lives on a circle with four digit prototypes. The blue arrow is the pull from the rest of the puzzle, standing in for injection and attention. At small β the mixture is almost flat and the pull decides where the cell goes. At large β each prototype digs its own well and the cell commits to the nearest one. Annealing β from small to large lets the constraints act first and commits late.

3 · A 4×4 Sudoku solved by descending one energy

The same energy at puzzle scale, with weights set by hand rather than trained. Each of the 16 cells is a unit vector in four dimensions, with digit prototypes e₁…e₄. The evidence term pins the clues. One repulsive attention head over each cell's row, column and box pushes peers apart. The memory pulls every cell toward a digit, with β annealed. The update is the plain gradient step z ← Expz(−η grad E). The bars in each cell show its soft assignment at the current β, and a digit turns solid once the cell commits.

4 · Restarts, and why the energy can choose between them

Restarting from new random states finds the solution more often. The hard part is choosing which halted state to trust. In SFPRM every halted state has a residual near zero, so the residual cannot rank them, and the anchor angle turned out to rank them badly. With an energy the choice is natural: a wrong board violates a constraint or sits off the prototypes, so it ends higher. Picking the lowest-energy restart then works whenever any restart found the answer.

5 · What the trained models show

The same picture appears in the trained models: SFPRM against the energy model with the codebook readout on Sudoku, 500 out-of-distribution test puzzles with fewer clues than in training. First, the spectrum of the Jacobian at halted states, damped by a step η of your choice. Every eigenvalue of the energy model is real, as the theory requires, while 95% of SFPRM's form complex pairs at the state shown. The energy model's eigenvalues spread far along the real axis, so it needs a small step, which is the stiffness from figure 1.

Second, accuracy against the number of restarts. For SFPRM only rules without an energy are available. For the energy model with the codebook readout, the lowest-energy restart tracks the oracle: from 75% to 99% out of distribution on 4×4 (oracle 99%) and from 10% to 88% on 6×6 (oracle 90%), with 64 restarts. The first energy model, without the codebook readout, shows what happens when the energy does not rank answers: selection makes things worse.
